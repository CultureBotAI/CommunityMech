Industrial Bioleaching Reactor Consortium

Community ID CommunityMech:000035
Ecological State ENGINEERED
Environment bioreactor
ENVO:00002123

An industrial-scale moderately thermophilic acidophilic microbial consortium operating in continuous and batch bioreactors for metal extraction from sulfide ores and concentrates. This consortium represents a unique system with complete genomic characterization of six key organisms: Acidiplasma sp., Acidithiobacillus caldus, Cuniculiplasma divulgatum, Ferroplasma acidiphilum, Ferroplasma sp., and Thermoplasmatales archaeon. The community operates at moderate thermophilic temperatures (40-50°C) and extremely low pH (1.5-2.0), oxidizing ferrous iron and reduced sulfur compounds to generate ferric iron (Fe³⁺) and sulfuric acid for dissolving chalcopyrite (CuFeS₂) and other metal sulfides. Comparative analysis across reactor modes reveals distinct population dynamics, with Acidithiobacillus caldus predominating early, Sulfobacillus acidophilus predominating in the middle stage, and Ferroplasma thermophilum accounting for the greatest proportion in the final stage. The adapted consortium achieves exceptional copper extraction of 60.4% from 20% pulp density chalcopyrite concentrate in 25 days. Operating under controlled carbon sources (CO₂, molasses) and temperature gradients (40-50°C), this system demonstrates how operational parameters shape microbial community structure and metal recovery efficiency. The availability of six complete reference genomes (PRJNA976529) enables genomic-level investigation of biooxidation mechanisms, making this the most comprehensively characterized industrial bioleaching consortium for biotechnological applications in extractive metallurgy.

Initial Acidithiobacillus caldus Predominance

The 2014 adapted chalcopyrite culture showed initial A. caldus predominance by qPCR at 20 percent pulp density. This positive population result does not identify A. caldus as a ferrous-iron oxidizer: the 2023 study describes sulfur-oxidizing potential for A. caldus BR_03, not its ownership of the A-plasma rusticyanin finding. The studies concern different communities. Relative predominance does not establish the proposed pH, substrate-depletion or competitive mechanism of succession.

Middle-Stage Sulfobacillus acidophilus Predominance

The 2014 20-percent-pulp chalcopyrite experiment reports S. acidophilus, rather than A. caldus, as predominant in the middle stage. The positive qPCR population result does not selectively establish a mixotrophic transition, superior iron-oxidation efficiency, a molasses response or competitive niche partitioning. Those causal mechanisms cannot be assigned from the succession observation alone.

Ferroplasmaceae Iron-Oxidizing Potential

Adapted Chalcopyrite Consortium Copper Extraction

After more than two years of serial adaptation, the 2014 mixed culture extracted 60.4 percent of copper from chalcopyrite at 20 percent pulp density in 25 days. This measured consortium outcome is retained. The two canonical participants listed here are a supported subset, not the complete culture: the paper also reports F. thermophilum, which is not represented by the canonical F. acidiphilum/Acidiplasma entries. Ferric-iron regeneration is a proposed process context, not an A. caldus iron-oxidation loop or a selectively measured contribution of the 2023 MAGs. Resolve the exact roster and missing valid regeneration structure under #1509.

Leptospirillum Detection During Pulp-Density Adaptation

The 2014 culture contained L. ferriphilum before adaptation above 4 percent pulp density, after which it was not detected. This positive culture-composition comparison does not prove extinction or isolate pulp density from correlated organics, reagents and adaptation history. Temperature-Structured A-Plasma and Acidiplasma Populations

The 2023 reactor comparison reports increasing Acidiplasma relative abundance from 40 to 50 C and higher A-plasma abundance at 45 C. Preserve this operationally associated population pattern without labeling it a demonstrated pairwise niche interaction. Reactor mode and carbon source also matter; single reactor runs per condition and relative profiles do not isolate competitive mechanisms or assign absolute growth rates to each group.
