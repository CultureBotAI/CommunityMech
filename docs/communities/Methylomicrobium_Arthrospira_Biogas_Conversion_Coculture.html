Methylomicrobium-Arthrospira Biogas Conversion Coculture

Community ID CommunityMech:000496
Ecological State ENGINEERED
Environment laboratory biogas-fed photobioreactor coculture — ontology label: laboratory environment
ENVO:01001405 · HabitatMech ↗

A defined synthetic binary methanotroph-photoautotroph coculture pairing Methylomicrobium buryatense 5GB1 (currently represented in NCBI Taxonomy as Methylotuvimicrobium buryatense 5G) with the oxygenic cyanobacterium Arthrospira platensis (Limnospira platensis) for simultaneous conversion of biogas methane and carbon dioxide without external oxygen supply. In illuminated closed bioreactors supplied with CH4/CO2/N2 feeding gas in 90% Zarrouk plus 10% NMS2 medium (pH 8.7-9) and refed every 24 h, photosynthetic O2 generated by A. platensis is completely consumed by M. buryatense 5GB1 to oxidize CH4 and release CO2 for cyanobacterial fixation. Because enhanced coculture growth of both partners exceeds what in situ O2 and CO2 exchange alone explains in dynamic flux balance analysis, genome-scale metabolic modeling (SteadyCom and DynamiCom coupling iMb5G(B1) and NIES-39) predicts emergent cross-feeding of ammonium, succinate, pyruvate, formate, glutamine, and glutamate.

In Situ Oxygen Provision for Methane Oxidation

CROSS_FEEDING

Under illumination in closed biogas-fed bioreactors without external O2 supply, Arthrospira platensis performs oxygenic photosynthesis and supplies dissolved O2 in situ to Methylomicrobium buryatense 5GB1. No headspace O2 accumulates during the growth experiment because the O2 produced by A. platensis is completely consumed by M. buryatense 5GB1 to drive aerobic methane oxidation.

Reciprocal Carbon Dioxide Provision and Synergistic Coculture Growth

MUTUALISM

Methane oxidation by Methylomicrobium buryatense 5GB1 produces dissolved CO2 in situ that supplements inorganic carbon for Arthrospira platensis photosynthesis, establishing a mutualistic gas-exchange loop that supports simultaneous CH4 and CO2 conversion. However, dynamic flux balance analysis demonstrates that the enhanced growth observed for both species in coculture exceeds what in situ O2 and CO2 exchange alone can explain.

Predicted Emergent Cyanobacterial Ammonium and Organic Acid Cross-Feeding

CROSS_FEEDING

HYPOTHESIZED - genome-scale metabolic modeling (SteadyCom and DynamiCom) constrained by experimental growth and gas-exchange kinetics predicts that Arthrospira platensis exports ammonium, succinate, pyruvate, formate, and L-glutamate to Methylomicrobium buryatense 5GB1. DynamiCom predicts that after approximately 38 h (establishing the mutualistic relationship), A. platensis produces more pyruvate and glutamate while continuing to provide NH4+, succinate, and formate, and that after approximately 45 h M. buryatense 5GB1 switches from co-consuming medium nitrate and cyanobacterial ammonium to exclusively consuming cyanobacterial ammonium alongside increased glutamate exchange. Direct experimental metabolomic validation of these predicted fluxes remains outstanding.

Predicted Reciprocal Methanotroph Carbon and Glutamine Cross-Feeding

CROSS_FEEDING

HYPOTHESIZED - dynamic flux balance analysis (DynamiCom) and steady-state community modeling (SteadyCom) predict that once the mutualistic coculture relationship is established after approximately 38 h, Methylomicrobium buryatense 5GB1 produces increased citrate and L-glutamine while continuing to provide lactate and sucrose to Arthrospira platensis, complementing cyanobacterial ammonium and organic acid provision. Direct experimental metabolomic validation of these predicted fluxes remains outstanding.
