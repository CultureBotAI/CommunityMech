Ngawha Geothermal Acidic Springs Mercury Cycling Community

ENVO:00000051

An acidic warm spring microbial community from the Ngawha Geothermal Field (Northland Region, Aotearoa New Zealand) characterized by genome-resolved metagenomics and mercury speciation analysis. Two adjacent springs differ in mercury speciation, with dissolved total and methylated mercury concentrations among the highest reported from natural sources (250 to 16,000 ng/L total; 0.5 to 13.9 ng/L methylmercury). Sediment total mercury ranges from 1,274 to 7,000 ug/g. Despite ultrahigh mercury levels, the geothermal microbiome is unexpectedly diverse and dominated by acidophilic and mesophilic sulfur- and iron-cycling bacteria, mercury- and arsenic- resistant bacteria, and thermophilic and acidophilic archaea. A conceptual model integrates community structure and metagenomic potential with abiotic and biotic controls on Hg methylation, demethylation, and reduction to volatile Hg(0).

Acidic Warm-Spring Mercury Speciation Regime

Geochemical measurements characterize acidic warm Ngawha springs with high dissolved total mercury and methylmercury and high sediment mercury. These measured environmental pools constrain hypotheses about bioavailability, but total dissolved mercury is not synonymous with freely available Hg(II), and co-occurrence does not show that the regime caused the observed diversity.

Diverse Acidophile Guilds Under Ultrahigh Mercury

The sampled Ngawha microbiomes were unexpectedly diverse despite high mercury, with sulfur- and iron-cycling bacteria, Hg/As-resistance potential and acidophilic archaea; the two baths differed in composition. This metagenomic observation does not demonstrate temporal stability, realized niche partitioning, or a mercury-driven diversity increase.

Metabolites: mercury(2+) (CHEBI:16793), sulfur atom (CHEBI:26833)

Measured Gaseous Mercury With Unresolved Microbial Contribution

Gaseous Hg(0) was measured above Tiger and Cub Baths, alongside aqueous and sediment mercury measurements. The source also reports merA-based reduction potential. The measured atmospheric mercury is a positive environmental result, not an isolated microbial emission rate: deep geological inputs and abiotic redox processes also contribute, and their contributions were not separated from microbial reduction.

Metabolites: mercury(2+) (CHEBI:16793), mercury(0) (CHEBI:16170)

Sulfur-Iron-Coupled Mercury Cycle Model

HYPOTHESIZED - The conceptual model links sulfur/iron redox cycling and cinnabar precipitation/dissolution to mercury bioavailability, with potential methylation, demethylation and Hg(II) reduction. These coupled biotic and abiotic reactions are an evidence-informed model, not demonstrated interspecies syntrophy or a measured taxon-to-taxon mercury flux.

Knowledge gaps & discussions (1)

KNOWLEDGE_GAP OPEN Which measurements establish the proposed causal mechanism in this exact system?

Retain measured mercury pools, atmospheric Hg(0), diverse microbiomes and positive mer/hgc sequence evidence. The hgcA sequences could not be binned, MerA HMM hits included paralogs, and no hgcAB was recovered from resolved sulfate-reducing bins. Absence at one sequencing depth is not biological absence. Environmental concentrations and mer/hgc potential do not isolate microbial rates or prove temporal stability. The sulfur/iron/cinnabar coupling is proposed; selective activity assays and source partitioning are needed.
