Rifle Uranium-Reducing Community

Community ID CommunityMech:000060
Ecological State PERTURBED
Environment contaminated soil
ENVO:00002116

A microbial community stimulated for in situ bioremediation of uranium-contaminated groundwater at the Old Rifle site in Rifle, Colorado. This aquifer community demonstrates sequential reduction processes following acetate biostimulation at 1-3 mM concentrations. The community transitions through distinct metabolic phases: Phase I (days 0-50) dominated by Geobacter species (up to 89% of community) performing Fe(III) reduction coupled to U(VI) reduction, achieving 70% uranium removal within 50 days and reducing concentrations below the EPA treatment goal of 0.18 μM in some wells. Phase II (>50 days) shifts to sulfate-reducing bacteria including Desulfobacteraceae (45% by day 80), Desulfosporosinus, and Desulfotomaculum acetoxidans as Fe(III) becomes depleted. Geobacter metallireducens and Anaeromyxobacter dehalogenans reduce soluble U(VI) to insoluble U(IV), forming biogenic uraninite nanoparticles that immobilize uranium. Desulfovibrio species contribute to uranium reduction through enzymatic pathways while generating hydrogen sulfide that can chemically reduce uranium. This represents the first successful field demonstration of uranium bioremediation via stimulating subsurface Geobacter populations, providing an alternative to pump-and-treat remediation. Groundwater flow rate is 0.82 m/day, creating dynamic conditions where microbial metabolism must outpace uranium advection.

Geobacter Enrichment and Coincident Metal Reduction

During the initial Rifle field response to acetate amendment, Geobacter enrichment accompanied groundwater uranium loss and increasing Fe(II). This supports coincident community metal reduction, not a measured G. metallireducens-specific flux, a demonstrated cytochrome mechanism or cross-feeding between named members.

Initial Groundwater Uranium Removal

Groundwater U(VI) decreased during the initial acetate-amended field phase and fell below the prescribed treatment level in some monitoring wells. This is a measured removal outcome, not a direct characterization of 2-5 nm uraninite or a demonstrated Geobacter-Anaeromyxobacter mutualism. Later sulfate reduction coincided with increasing groundwater uranium, not sustained removal at the initial effectiveness.

Later Sulfate Reduction and Sulfide Accumulation

The later field phase showed sulfate loss and sulfide accumulation as sulfate-reducer-related sequences became predominant. Groundwater uranium increased during this transition. These observations do not establish sulfide-mediated U(VI) reduction, a uraninite/uranium-sulfide product or pairwise nutrient exchange in this field system.

Community Succession from Metal to Sulfate Reduction

The groundwater community shifted from Geobacter enrichment toward sulfate-reducer-related sequences during continued amendment. HYPOTHESIZED: depletion of accessible Fe(III) near the amendment zone and local acetate consumption may have favored this transition. The primary discussion calls this explanation a hypothesis requiring verification; succession alone does not establish reciprocal harm, exact species replacement or the sediment-attached community composition.

Metabolites: Fe(III) (CHEBI:29034), sulfate (CHEBI:16189)

Downstream Effects:
→ Later Sulfate Reduction and Sulfide Accumulation

PARTIAL: the shift toward sulfate-reducer-related populations is proposed to accompany a change in the dominant acetate-oxidizing process, contributing to sulfate consumption and sulfide accumulation. The Fe(III)-depletion and donor-consumption explanation remains a field hypothesis, not independently established mediation or continued uranium immobilization.
