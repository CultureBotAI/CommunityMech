Rhodopseudomonas palustris CGA676 and Escherichia coli PFM2/PFM2 ΔpurH Adenine Competition Coculture

ENVO:01001405 · HabitatMech ↗

Defined three-strain synthetic bacterial mutual-invasion competition coculture pairing phototrophic nitrogen-fixing and adenine-excreting Rhodopseudomonas palustris CGA676 (~10^6 CFU/mL) with prototrophic Escherichia coli PFM2 and engineered adenine-auxotrophic Escherichia coli PFM2 ΔpurH (~10^6 CFU/mL total initial E. coli density across initial frequencies from 0.01 to 0.99) in anaerobic M9-derived coculture medium (MDC) supplemented with 25 mM glucose under N2 headspace and constant illumination at 30 degrees C by Chuang et al. 2026 (PMID:42117636). Constructed to directly test the Black Queen Hypothesis prediction that adenine cross-feeding from R. palustris should confer a fitness advantage on E. coli purine-biosynthesis loss-of-function mutants, 5-day invasion-from-rare competition assays demonstrated that E. coli ΔpurH did not increase in frequency relative to ancestral prototrophic PFM2 across the 0.01–0.99 initial frequency gradient (Fig. S1C), because partner-supplied adenine represses purH transcription in prototrophic PFM2 to 20% of monoculture levels (Figure 3C), lowering ancestral gene expression cost and eliminating selective advantage for purH deletion.

Ammonium and Adenine Cross-Feeding from R. palustris CGA676 to Prototrophic E. coli PFM2 and Auxotrophic PFM2 ΔpurH

CROSS_FEEDING

In anaerobic MDC medium containing 25 mM glucose as the sole carbon source and N2 as the sole nitrogen source, phototrophic Rhodopseudomonas palustris CGA676 fixes N2 into excreted ammonium (NH4+) and externalizes the purine adenine at levels sufficient to support an E. coli adenine auxotroph (ΔpurH) population alongside prototrophic E. coli PFM2, while both E. coli strains ferment glucose into organic acids that provide essential carbon to R. palustris.

Repression of Prototrophic E. coli purH Expression Eliminating Fitness Advantage of PFM2 ΔpurH in Mutual-Invasion Competition

COMPETITION

In 5-day mutual-invasion competition assays across initial frequencies targeting 0.01 to 0.99 in coculture with R. palustris CGA676, engineered adenine-auxotrophic E. coli PFM2 ΔpurH did not increase in frequency relative to prototrophic E. coli PFM2 (Fig. S1C). Because adenine cross-fed from R. palustris CGA676 represses purH transcript levels in prototrophic E. coli PFM2 to 20% of monoculture levels (Figure 3C), the ancestral strain lowers its purine synthesis gene expression cost without gene loss, eliminating any competitive fitness advantage for PFM2 ΔpurH.

Knowledge gaps & discussions (1)

EMERGING_HYPOTHESIS OPEN Why did purine biosynthesis mutations emerge at low frequency in earlier cocultures pairing R. palustris with Escherichia coli MG1655 (Figure 4A) whereas E. coli PFM2 ΔpurH gained no competitive advantage over prototrophic PFM2 in coculture with R. palustris CGA676?

PMID:42117636 shows that E. coli MG1655 carries an rph frameshift mutation that causes pyrimidine starvation and growth inhibition in the presence of adenine, which is alleviated by deleting purH (Figure 4B); in contrast, E. coli PFM2 has a repaired rph+ allele and represses purH transcription 5-fold in coculture, eliminating both adenine toxicity and BQH gene-cost selection.
