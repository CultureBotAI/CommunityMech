Neocallimastix-Methanobrevibacter Xylanolytic Coculture

A defined anaerobic rumen-derived coculture in which the xylan-degrading fungus Neocallimastix frontalis PNK2 is grown with the hydrogenotrophic archaeon Methanobrevibacter smithii on xylan. The methanogen removes fermentation products through interspecies hydrogen transfer, shifting fungal metabolism toward acetate formation and increasing xylan utilization and extracellular hemicellulase activities.

Interspecies Hydrogen Transfer During Xylan Fermentation

SYNTROPHY

In the N. frontalis PNK2-M. smithii coculture, hydrogen and formate did not accumulate, acetate formation increased and lactate decreased, consistent with the reported interspecies hydrogen transfer and methanogenesis. M. smithii alone did not utilize xylan or supply the assayed extracellular hydrolase activities. These results support metabolic coupling without establishing obligate dependence of fungal growth on this partner.

→ Methanogen-Stimulated Xylan Utilization

PARTIAL - Methanogenic removal of fermentation products shifts fungal metabolism toward acetate and is proposed to support the observed increase in xylan-derived sugar utilization. The coculture comparison supports the positive effect, but does not isolate hydrogen removal from growth and other partner-associated changes; utilization is distinct from polymer depolymerization.

→ Enhanced Extracellular Hemicellulase Activities

HYPOTHESIZED - Methanogenic product removal is proposed to improve fungal sugar uptake, reducing extracellular xylose accumulation and catabolite repression of hemicellulases. This supplies a mechanistic path to the measured enzyme increase, not merely a shared coculture context; the authors leave control of sugar uptake unresolved.

Methanogen-Stimulated Xylan Utilization

SYNTROPHY

Coculture with M. smithii increased the rate and extent of xylan utilization by N. frontalis PNK2 relative to fungal monoculture, with stimulation maintained during an 18-day experiment. Increased utilization does not imply a corresponding increase in polymer depolymerization: N. frontalis already degraded most xylan in both conditions, and the source distinguishes degradation from uptake and fermentation of released sugars.

Enhanced Extracellular Hemicellulase Activities

SYNTROPHY

Coculture increased extracellular xylanase, alpha-L-arabinofuranosidase and beta-D-xylosidase specific activities, normalized to fungal protein rather than explained solely by greater biomass. Magnitudes depended on fungus, enzyme, substrate and sampling time; the abstract maxima summarize a comparison panel, not uniform fold changes for N. frontalis PNK2. Cell-bound endopeptidase was not increased and alpha-D-glucosidase was little affected.

Preserve the directly measured coculture utilization and enzyme gains, as well as hydrogen/formate and fermentation-product changes. Distinguish polymer degradation from utilization and protein-normalized enzyme activity from biomass.
