A defined three-strain lactic-acid-bacterial triculture assembled from Parmigiano Reggiano natural whey starter (NWS) isolates in inoculation set 3. Cristofolini et al. 2026 combined Lactobacillus helveticus C001.15, Streptococcus thermophilus RBC06, and Lactobacillus delbrueckii subsp. lactis C5I3 in a 1:1:1 milk triculture at 2 x 10^7 CFU/mL total inoculum in 40 mL partially skimmed UHT milk at 42 degrees C for 160 h to evaluate how NWS partner strains modulate milk acidification and D-/L-lactate enantiomer accumulation when pairing the set 1 L. helveticus C001.15 and S. thermophilus RBC06 isolates with the more vigorous set 2 L. delbrueckii subsp. lactis C5I3 strain (PMID:41859451).

Set 3 triculture milk acidification and total lactate enhancement

The set 3 C001.15/RBC06/C5I3 1:1:1 triculture displayed the highest maximum acidification rate (umax), highest maximum fermentative efficiency (Delta pHmax, Figure 7B), and highest total lactic acid production compared with the set 3 monocultures and 1:1 pairwise co-cultures (p < 0.05) at constant total inoculum (2 x 10^7 CFU/mL). These community-level fermentation readouts establish enhanced mixed-culture acidification and total lactate accumulation without isolating per-strain cell counts or specific exchanged metabolites.

RBC06-containing pairwise acidification and lactate stimulation in Set 3

In set 3 pairwise 1:1 co-cultures containing Streptococcus thermophilus RBC06 (RBC06 + C5I3 and RBC06 + C001.15), maximum acidification rates (umax), maximum acidification efficiency (Delta pHmax), and total lactate concentrations significantly exceeded those of the corresponding monocultures (p < 0.05). In the RBC06 + C001.15 pair, L-lactate accounted for 86.73% of total lactate, whereas in the RBC06 + C5I3 pair production was dominated by D-lactate. These two-member phenotypes are preserved as separate pairwise comparison outcomes without asserting a subset-to-triculture causal arrow.

C001.15-C5I3 reduced acidification and D-lactate-majority pairwise subset

Co-inoculation of L. helveticus C001.15 with L. delbrueckii subsp. lactis C5I3 in set 3 resulted in lower maximum acidification rate (umax) and lower maximum acidification efficiency (Delta pHmax) than the corresponding monocultures and yielded significantly lower total lactic acid than the set 3 triculture and the St + Lh and St + Ld co-cultures (Figure 8). Unlike the set 1 C001.15 + T1104 pair (67.42% L-lactate), D-lactate accounted for the majority (56.63%) of total lactate in the set 3 C001.15 + C5I3 co-culture, indicating that L. delbrueckii subsp. lactis C5I3 remained metabolically active despite reduced bulk acidification.

Set 3 triculture balanced D-/L-lactate accumulation relative to C5I3 monoculture

At the 160 h fermentation endpoint, D-lactate and L-lactate were produced in comparable amounts in the set 3 C001.15/RBC06/C5I3 triculture. Notably, whereas sets 1 and 2 showed significantly higher D-lactate in tricultures than in their corresponding Ld monocultures (p < 0.05), set 3 did not show a statistically significant triculture D-lactate increase over the C5I3 monoculture alone, reflecting strain-combination specificity when C5I3 is paired with C001.15 and RBC06.
