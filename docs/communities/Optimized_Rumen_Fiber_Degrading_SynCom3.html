A five-strain synthetic rumen bacterial consortium optimized from dairy-cattle culturomics isolates to increase fiber degradation of a total-mixed-ration substrate. The Streptococcus equinus 5640, Lactobacillus delbrueckii 5812, Proteiniphilum acetatigenes 6076, Enterococcus lactis 6088, and Petrimonas mucosa 6360 SynCom 3 derivative was dose-optimized by a Box-Behnken in vitro design and then dosed into fistulated Huaxi cattle as a mixed bacterial suspension.

In situ cattle rumen fermentation shift

Biological Processes:

  • carbohydrate metabolic process (GO:0005975)

Evidence

  • doi:10.1186/s40168-025-02331-3 - SUPPORT (IN_VIVO)
    "The in situ feeding trial demonstrated that the supplementation of the SynCom significantly altered the rumen fermentation profile and enhanced feed fiber degradation in Huaxi cattle. The ruminal degradation rates of DM, NDF, and ADF exhibited a positive trend in the TRE group, with increases of 2.61%, 7.81%, and 11.47%, respectively, at 72 h compared to the CON group, although these differences were not statistically significant (Fig.  8 A–C, P > 0.05). SynCom supplementation profoundly influenced ruminal VFA production. While the total VFA concentration in the TRE group was significantly lower than in the CON group at 12 h (Fig.  8 F, P = 0.0011), it increased progressively over time, becoming significantly higher in the TRE group at 24 h (P = 0.0196) and 48 h (P = 0.0001), and remaining 10.80% higher at 72 h (P > 0.05). This shift was driven by a significantly elevated acetate concentration in the TRE group at 24, 48, and 72 h (Fig.  8 G, P < 0.05), with no significant difference observed in propionate levels. Consequently, the A:P ratio was significantly elevated in the treatment group at 48 h and 72 h (Fig.  8 M, P < 0.05). The concentrations of butyrate, isobutyrate, and isovalerate in the TRE group were also significantly higher than in the CON group at 24 and 48 h (Fig.  8 I, J, and L, P < 0.05). In contrast, valerate concentration in the TRE group was significantly lower than in the CON group at 48 h (Fig.  8 K, P = 0.0013) and 72 h (Fig.  8 K, P < 0.0001). Concomitantly, the NH3-N concentration was significantly lower in the TRE group at 48 h and 72 h (Fig.  8 E, P< 0.0001 and P = 0.0034, respectively), implying improved nitrogen utilization by the rumen microbiota."

"Glass bottles (100 mL) were prepared with 0.75 g of ground TMR substrate (Table S4), 45 mL of pre-warmed fermentation medium (Table S5), and 15 mL of filtration-sterilized rumen fluid. Each bottle was inoculated with 1 mL of 1 × 107 CFU/mL of the following strains: B. subtilis 5632, S. equinus 5640, S. pluranimalium 5823, S. infantarius 6074, L. delbrueckii 5812, P. mucosa 6360, S. condimenti 5845, E. gallinarum 6392, A. glycerinitolerans 6345, E. lactis 6088, C. pasinlerensis 5847, L. pontis 5897, and P. acetatigenes 6076. All bacterial suspensions underwent centrifugation (12,000 × g, 10 min) to remove the culture medium, after which the resulting cell pellets were resuspended in an equivalent volume of sterile water. 1 mL of sterile water was utilized as the negative control (CON). All bottles were sealed with a rubber stopper and aluminum crimp cap immediately after loading and were incubated at 39 °C for 48 h"
