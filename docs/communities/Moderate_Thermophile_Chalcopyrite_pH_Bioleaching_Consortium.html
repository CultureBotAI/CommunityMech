Moderate Thermophile Chalcopyrite pH-Regulated Bioleaching Consortium

Community ID CommunityMech:000487
Ecological State ENGINEERED
Environment bioreactor
ENVO:00002123 · HabitatMech ↗

A defined mixed culture of moderate thermophiles comprising Sulfobacillus thermosulfidooxidans, Acidithiobacillus caldus, and Ferroplasma thermophilum used for chalcopyrite bioleaching across initial pH values of 1.0, 2.0, and 3.0, exhibiting optimal leaching kinetics at initial pH 2.0 and stage-specific succession from early free-living S. thermosulfidooxidans and mineral-attached A. caldus to late-phase F. thermophilum dominance (doi:10.1007/s13213-019-01453-y).

Early Planktonic-Attached Niche Partitioning in Chalcopyrite Oxidation

SYNTROPHY

During early chalcopyrite bioleaching, planktonic Sulfobacillus thermosulfidooxidans and mineral-attached Acidithiobacillus caldus partition iron and sulfur oxidation across free and attached niches via 4Fe-4S ferredoxin and sulfate adenylyltransferase expression.

Source Taxon: Sulfobacillus thermosulfidooxidans

Participating Taxa: Sulfobacillus thermosulfidooxidans, Acidithiobacillus caldus

Metabolites: iron(2+) (CHEBI:29033 · MediaIngredientMech · CMMMech), sulfate (CHEBI:16189 · MediaIngredientMech · CMMMech)

Biological Processes:

  • sulfur oxidation (GO:0019417 · PathwayMech)
  • electron transport chain (GO:0022900 · PathwayMech)
Downstream Effects:
→ Late-Phase Ferroplasma Succession and pH-Regulated Iron-Sulfur Oxidation

Early-stage chalcopyrite bioleaching dominated by free Sulfobacillus thermosulfidooxidans and mineral-attached Acidithiobacillus caldus transitions in the later phase to Ferroplasma thermophilum predominance alongside pH-dependent iron and sulfur oxidation gene expression.

Late-Phase Ferroplasma Succession and pH-Regulated Iron-Sulfur Oxidation

NICHE_PARTITIONING

Ferroplasma thermophilum succeeds early bacterial oxidizers to become predominant in the later phase of chalcopyrite bioleaching, with pH-dependent transcriptional regulation of iron/sulfur oxidation genes in S. thermosulfidooxidans and F. thermophilum alongside pH-stable expression in A. caldus.

Source Taxon: Ferroplasma thermophilum

Participating Taxa: Sulfobacillus thermosulfidooxidans, Acidithiobacillus caldus, Ferroplasma thermophilum
