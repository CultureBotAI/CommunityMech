A perturbed indigenous microbial community from vanadium-titanium magnetite mine tailings in Panzhihua, Sichuan, China, the world's largest V-Ti magnetite deposit and largest vanadium production city. This community comprises nitrogen-fixing rhizobia (Bradyrhizobium pachyrhizi, Rhizobium species), metal-reducing bacteria, and indigenous microbiota that facilitate phytoremediation of polymetallic tailings containing vanadium (340 mg/kg), titanium, iron, copper, nickel, manganese, and zinc. The Panzhihua deposit has generated approximately 570 million tons of tailing slurries from mining activities, creating a massive contamination challenge requiring sustainable bioremediation. The indigenous microbial community has adapted to extreme metal stress through nitrogen fixation coupled with plant growth promotion, enabling successful legume-based phytoremediation with Pongamia pinnata and Leucaena leucocephala. Bradyrhizobium species demonstrate exceptional heavy metal tolerance while maintaining nitrogen-fixing capacity (increasing plant nitrogen content by 10-145%), promoting plant growth through IAA production, and facilitating metal phytoextraction (enhancing Cu and Ni uptake by 600%). Metal-reducing bacteria contribute to vanadium detoxification through dissimilatory V(V) reduction to less soluble V(IV), decreasing vanadium bioavailability and mobilization risk. The community drives transformation of soil organic carbon, increasing active carbon components and carbon transformation enzymes during revegetation, improving overall tailings soil quality. This system represents a critical model for coupling nitrogen fixation, metal tolerance, and phytoremediation in polymetallic mine tailings, addressing both resource recovery and environmental remediation challenges in the world's largest vanadium-titanium deposit.

Pongamia Nodulation and Nitrogen-Enrichment Phenotypes

Bradyrhizobium isolates from Pongamia pinnata nodules in Panzhihua tailings formed nodules and increased plant nitrogen and growth in separate nitrogen-free vermiculite pot assays. Results identify PP76 and other Bradyrhizobium isolates, while PP1 belongs to Rhizobium in the Results and must not be assigned to B. pachyrhizi. The abstract assigns twelve isolates to B. pachyrhizi, but species-level trees and labels are inconsistent. The canonical species endpoint therefore represents the authors provisional group assignment, not a verified PP1 identity. These are positive inoculation phenotypes with nifH evidence, not a measured ammonium-transfer flux, V-Fe nitrogenase demonstration, or mutualism between bacterial species. The same study also reports separate metal-tolerance phenotypes for PP76 and other isolates; internal Cd/Zn inconsistencies preclude unqualified metal-specific numerical assignments here.

Metal Tolerance of Cultured Tailings Bacillus

The 2014 Panzhihua bulk-soil isolate screen found multiple-metal tolerance among Bacillus isolates, including five strains tolerant to all six tested metals. These were isolate growth assays, not a demonstration that Bacillus transfers metabolites to rhizobia or enhances plant metal uptake. Keep this result separate from the 2023 nodule-isolate screen and the 2018 S. saheli YH1/Leucaena system, which reduced plant metal uptake. The previously assigned B. liaoningense phytoextraction percentages do not belong to B. pachyrhizi or to the cited YH1 experiment. Efflux, sequestration, ACC degradation and metal-redox mechanisms were not isolated by the tolerance assay.

Ochrobactrum Isolate Growth-Promoter Production

The 2014 bulk-soil collection included thirteen isolates identified as Ochrobactrum intermedium. In that collection, six Ochrobactrum isolates produced more than 60 micrograms/mL IAA, and KT80 had the highest reported IAA concentration, 83.05 micrograms/mL; two Ochrobactrum isolates were among the siderophore producers. The main-text wording does not unambiguously allocate IAA/siderophore co-production to both isolates. These are in-vitro production phenotypes, not demonstrated metabolite delivery to Bradyrhizobium, ACC deaminase activity, metal reduction or a community-resilience mechanism. The 2023 Ochrobactrum nodule isolates are a different collection with different species assignments; they cannot establish O. intermedium symbiotic nitrogen fixation.
