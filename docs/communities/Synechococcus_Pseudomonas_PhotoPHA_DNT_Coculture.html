Synechococcus-Pseudomonas Phototrophic PHA and DNT Coculture

Community ID CommunityMech:000167
Ecological State ENGINEERED
Environment illuminated laboratory photobioreactor coculture — ontology label: laboratory environment
ENVO:01001405

A synthetic, light-driven two-species coculture pairing engineered Synechococcus elongatus PCC 7942 cscB with engineered Pseudomonas putida strains that consume cyanobacterial sucrose. The cyanobacterium fixes CO2 and exports sucrose under salt/IPTG induction, while P. putida converts the transferred carbon into polyhydroxyalkanoates (PHA) and, in a related engineered implementation, transforms the industrial pollutant 2,4-dinitrotoluene. This platform is relevant to DOE carbon utilization, bioproduct formation, and contaminant bioremediation because it couples photosynthetic CO2 fixation to heterotrophic biocatalysis in a controlled laboratory community.

Photosynthetic Sucrose Cross-Feeding

CROSS_FEEDING

S. elongatus cscB supplies photosynthetically derived sucrose to engineered P. putida partners. This record spans distinct implementations: cscAB PHA production, cscRABY and its nitrate-blind derivative, and EM173-derived DNT transformation. Their outcomes and growth-feedback assays are not interchangeable strain-level observations.

Downstream Effects:
→ Heterotrophic PHA Accumulation

Cyanobacterial sucrose supplies carbon for P. putida PHA accumulation in the production assays. Polymer accumulation depends on the tested strain and nitrogen regime; the nitrate-blind comparison measures final accumulation, not a production-rate increase.

→ DNT Biotransformation with Photosynthetic Carbon Input

In the separate EM173-derived DNT implementation, sucrose from encapsulated cyanobacteria supports heterotrophic growth and metabolism during DNT biotransformation. This does not equate pollutant disappearance with complete mineralization.

Heterotrophic PHA Accumulation

P. putida cscAB accumulated medium-chain-length PHA from cyanobacterial sucrose, with 3-hydroxydecanoate the most abundant monomer. A separate cscRABY nitrate-blind study reported an 8.8-fold increase in final PHA accumulation relative to its nitrate-consuming reference, not a measured rate increase. These production phenotypes are not additional cross-feeding events and do not establish PHB homopolymer identity.

DNT Biotransformation with Photosynthetic Carbon Input

The EM173-derived P. putida EM.DNT.S partner transformed 2,4-DNT in coculture with alginate-encapsulated S. elongatus CscB. Oxidative-intermediate measurements support pathway activity, whereas disappearance from solution also includes adsorption and reductive transformation. Complete mineralization was not directly established; the paper gives a model-based estimate. The same implementation accumulated PHA under nitrogen depletion, without proving a PHB homopolymer composition.

Growth-Promoting Partner Feedback

MUTUALISM

In the cscRABY study, P. putida promoted cyanobacterial growth and prevented photobleaching in tested contexts while receiving photosynthetic carbon. MUTUALISM here denotes that conditional carbon-supported partnership, not a universal fitness response: P. putida growth was not improved relative to matched external-sucrose controls. PARTIAL: additional molecular exchange and the mechanism of growth promotion remain hypotheses; mixed-cell omics and transporter regulation do not identify a transferred mediator. Sucrose is not a reverse donation.

Knowledge gaps & discussions (1)

KNOWLEDGE_GAP OPEN Which mechanism causes cyanobacterial growth promotion in the cscRABY coculture?

The primary study supports conditional cyanobacterial growth benefit and carbon supply to Pseudomonas, while matched external-sucrose controls show no heterotroph growth advantage. Transport regulation and mixed-cell metabolomics suggest additional interactions but do not resolve donor-recipient flux or distinguish a specific mediator from growth-state differences. Do not transfer this feedback to the cscAB or DNT implementation without exact-system evidence.
