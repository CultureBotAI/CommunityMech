Four-Species Pathogen Community Under DMS3vir Phage Predation

Community ID CommunityMech:000467
Ecological State ENGINEERED
Environment laboratory batch culture
ENVO:01001405

An artificial four-species bacterial community — Pseudomonas aeruginosa PA14, Acinetobacter baumannii, Staphylococcus aureus, and Burkholderia cenocepacia — propagated for 10 days in lysogeny broth with and without the lytic phage DMS3vir, which infects P. aeruginosa through its Type IV pilus. Without phage, P. aeruginosa outcompetes the other three species. Adding DMS3vir drives P. aeruginosa nearly extinct and releases A. baumannii into dominance, while maintaining community diversity. The experiment is fully factorial over all 1-, 2-, 3-, and 4-species combinations and over two P. aeruginosa clones (wild type and an isogenic CRISPR-KO), which lets the record separate the community consequences of phage predation from the consequences of which resistance mechanism evolves: the two resistance routes gave largely indistinguishable community dynamics, and Type IV pilus mutation became dominant even where CRISPR-Cas was functional. This is a one-phage-on-many-bacteria (1-N) system in the sense of doi:10.1146/annurev-virology-100424-123645 — a single phage population against a bacterial community — and the record exists to carry the community-level consequences of phage predation rather than the phage-host pair itself. Its modelling result is curated as a negative one: generalised Lotka-Volterra coefficients fitted to pairwise cocultures failed to predict the 3- and 4-species dynamics, so the assembled community is not reconstructible from its pairwise parts.

Knowledge gaps & discussions (3)

CURATION_TODO OPEN Should CommunityMech represent time-resolved composition, or keep trajectories in linked datasets and carry only the qualitative dynamic claim as an interaction?

The curated finding here is a trajectory — P. aeruginosa falls and A. baumannii rises over 10 days — but TaxonomicComposition carries a single abundance per member with no time axis, so `abundance_level` had to be set to the phage-free baseline and the trajectory written into interaction descriptions instead. A consumer reading abundances alone sees P. aeruginosa DOMINANT and A. baumannii MINOR, which is the pre-phage state and the opposite of this record's point. What would resolve it is a decision about whether CommunityMech represents time-series composition at all, or deliberately keeps trajectories in linked datasets and carries only the qualitative dynamic claim in `ecological_interactions`. This record is a good test case either way: the underlying per-timepoint densities are published.

Attaches to: taxonomy

OPEN_QUESTION OPEN Would a longer run of this community show rotating dominance, which would justify KILL_THE_WINNER here rather than the weaker LYTIC_INFECTION typing?

The diversity-maintenance result reads like kill-the-winner — predation on the standing dominant stops any one species excluding the rest — but the experiment runs 10 days and phage is driven extinct partway through, so what is observed is one competitive release, not turnover. The stronger value requires repeated or alternating dominance, so it is deliberately not used. A. baumannii is not itself knocked back after rising, and whether it would be under a longer run with persisting phage is the question that would settle this.

CURATION_TODO OPEN Where should a phage-resistance mechanism (receptor mutation versus defence system) and its fitness cost be recorded, now that prose in an interaction description is the only option?

Two curated claims here are about phage resistance — that Type IV pilus mutation outbeat CRISPR-Cas as the realised route, and that its fitness cost is the proposed reason P. aeruginosa never recovers. Both had to be written as COMPETITION interactions with the content in prose, because the schema has no resistance-mechanism or fitness-cost slot. If more phage records are curated this becomes a recurring loss, since receptor identity versus defence-system identity is the distinction that governs whether a phage cocktail works.
