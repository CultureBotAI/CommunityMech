High-Ammonia Biogas 0B Butyrate-Oxidizing Enrichment

A thermophilic, high-ammonia syntrophic butyrate-oxidizing enrichment culture maintained in yeast-extract-free 0B medium after derivation from a propionate-fed biogas reactor community. The enrichment was inoculated from a laboratory bioreactor that originated from large-scale food- and slaughterhouse-waste digestate, then followed as a single long-running, butyrate-fed anaerobic culture at 51 C for 972 days. Amplicon and metagenomic analyses recovered MAG1, a putative novel ammonia-tolerant syntrophic butyrate oxidizer proposed as Candidatus Syntrophothermus ammoniitolerans, together with a putative syntrophic acetate-oxidizing MAG related to Candidatus Thermosyntrophaceticus schinkii and hydrogenotrophic Methanoculleus and Methanothermobacter MAGs that likely close the network by consuming formate or H2 and producing methane.

Knowledge gaps & discussions (1)

KNOWLEDGE_GAP OPEN Does the moderate-quality Candidatus Thermosyntrophaceticus-like MAG2 actively perform syntrophic acetate oxidation in the 0B enrichment, and what is the division of hydrogen versus formate transfer among the MAG1, MAG2, Methanoculleus, and Methanothermobacter MAGs?

The 0B enrichment couples butyrate loss to methane formation, but MAG2 was reconstructed at moderate quality and a complete Wood-Ljungdahl pathway was inferred from a close relative rather than completely annotated in MAG2 itself.
