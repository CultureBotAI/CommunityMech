m-CAFEs Brachypodium Reduced Complexity Consortia

Community ID CommunityMech:000078
Ecological State ENGINEERED
Environment Brachypodium distachyon rhizosphere — ontology label: rhizosphere
ENVO:00005801

A systematic framework for assembling rhizosphere-derived Reduced Complexity Consortia (RCC) from Brachypodium distachyon roots grown in field soil from Angelo Coast Range Reserve, California. Enriched co-localized microbes with carbon substrates mimicking root exudates, generating 768 enrichments. Transferred every 3 or 7 days for 10 generations to develop fast and slow-growing reduced complexity communities. Keystone taxa identified within these networks belong to genera with plant growth-promoting traits. Tested consortia demonstrated high stability and reproducibility, assuring successful revival from glycerol stocks.

Root Exudate-Mimicking Enrichment Assembly

A multiplexed enrichment experiment varied root-associated inocula, carbon conditions and 3-day versus 7-day transfer intervals to generate a collection of Brachypodium-derived RCC. It is not one fixed consortium of the ten listed representative taxa. The abiotic selection treatments are experimental inputs, not direct evidence that species partition niches or reduce competition.

Downstream Effects:
→ Carbon-Source ASV Sorting

Changing the supplied carbon condition alters the ASV composition of enrichment cultures, with inoculum source accounted for in comparisons. This experimentally imposed selection does not demonstrate reciprocal niche partitioning or consumption of distinct substrates by each named organism.

→ Transfer-interval ASV abundance classification

Changing serial-transfer interval alters which ASVs are relatively enriched at later generations. The resulting 3-day/7-day abundance classification is a response to the transfer regime, not a direct per-ASV growth-rate measurement.

Carbon-Source ASV Sorting

Carbon amendments selected different ASVs in inoculum-stratified comparisons. The listed participants are representative named taxa, not all enriched ASVs. These relative-abundance changes do not isolate substrate consumption, microbial cooperation or reduced competition as their mechanism.

Transfer-interval ASV abundance classification

For a fixed carbon source and original inoculum, ASVs more abundant after 7-day transfers than 3-day transfers were called slow growers, and the converse were called fast growers. The comparison used generations 6 and 9. These are operational relative-abundance classes, not measured isolate growth rates; transfer duration also changes other ecological conditions. The reported capacity of 1/10 R2A, asparagine and glucuronic acid to enrich both classes is retained. Network-hub genera are not substituted for the set of ASVs in this differential-abundance analysis.

Recurring association-network hubs

Adjacent-generation network comparisons identified recurring hub taxa, including Paenibacillus, Sphingomonas, Mucilaginibacter and Terriglobus. This is persistence of a statistical hub designation across sampled transfers, not a taxon-removal test of community stability or direct measurement of niche partitioning. Inferred importance and PGP traits known for related genera are not validated functions of every enriched ASV.

1/10 R2A Glycerol Stock Revival

Five selected 1/10 R2A RCC stocks were revived and serially propagated, with broadly consistent dominant genera and relative abundances in those tests. This does not establish universal revival of all initial enrichments, exact strain-level identity, or retention of every rare taxon. Burkholderia and Mesorhizobium were shared statistical hubs in the revived-stock networks, not demonstrated causes of revival. The original broad 1/10 R2A enrichments were less stable than most other carbon arms; that result is distinct from stability of these five selected stocks.

Knowledge gaps & discussions (1)

KNOWLEDGE_GAP OPEN Which inferred RCC hubs have experimentally necessary ecological functions beyond association centrality?

The study manipulates carbon conditions and transfer intervals and directly tests selected-stock revival, but hub identity is assigned from correlation and eigenvector centrality. Taxon-removal/rescue, direct growth measurements and strain-resolved functional tests are needed before asserting niche partitioning, causal stabilization or PGP activity. The source represents multiple enrichments and selected stocks, not a single fixed ten-member community.
