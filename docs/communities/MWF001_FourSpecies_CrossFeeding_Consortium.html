MWF001 Four-Species Cross-Feeding Consortium

ENVO:01001405

A defined synthetic model community of four bacterial strains originally isolated from toxic metal-working fluids: Agrobacterium tumefaciens MWF001, Comamonas testosteroni MWF001, Microbacterium liquefaciens MWF001, now classified as Microbacterium maritypicum, and Ochrobactrum anthropi MWF001, now classified as Brucella anthropi. Sulheim et al. assembled the four strains into M9 minimal media with chemically diverse single or paired carbon sources, followed individual members by colony counts on selective media across eight 72 h growth-and-dilution cycles, and found that metabolic byproduct cross-feeding and niche partitioning let all four members coexist in nearly every tested nutrient condition.

Cross-fed metabolic byproduct partitioning

CROSS_FEEDING

PARTIAL - partner spent-media growth and persistence of primary-carbon-source nonusers support byproduct cross-feeding in MWF001. All four strains grew better in partner spent medium than their own, but exact exchanged compounds and donor-recipient fluxes were not chemically resolved. Unchanged stationary OD does not establish complete primary-substrate depletion; the greater-than-10-percent residual-resource exclusion described in Methods applies to simulations, not an equivalent experimental chemical assay. Generic consumer-resource models support coexistence through byproduct availability and differential resource affinities, not exact four-strain pathways. Fresh-medium pair effects were positive or negative depending on partner and condition, so reciprocal benefit is not universal.

Participating Taxa: Agrobacterium tumefaciens MWF001, Comamonas testosteroni MWF001, Microbacterium liquefaciens MWF001, Ochrobactrum anthropi MWF001

Robust coexistence across simple nutrient environments

All four strains remained detectable through eight 72-hour growth-and-dilution cycles in 31 carbon-supplied conditions, including 16 single-resource environments. These finite observations support broad conditional persistence, not asymptotic stability or a measured niche-partitioning mechanism. Additional 5X-malate conditions lost Comamonas after the first transfer; Microbacterium did not persist without carbon. The initial batch panel used 1/100 transfers except 1/10 for citrate and inosine, with the first citrate transfer at 1/100. Separate four-day chemostats on four single carbon sources also retained all four strains. The canonical participants preserve the source roster and prior unresolved taxonomic mappings, not independently recertified identities.

Participating Taxa: Agrobacterium tumefaciens MWF001, Comamonas testosteroni MWF001, Microbacterium liquefaciens MWF001, Ochrobactrum anthropi MWF001

Knowledge gaps & discussions (2)

KNOWLEDGE_GAP OPEN Resolve the exact metabolic byproducts exchanged by Agrobacterium, Comamonas, Microbacterium, and Ochrobactrum/Brucella in each carbon-source context.

The paper supports cross-feeding of partner-derived spent-media metabolites and uses a consumer-resource model to interpret the four-species coexistence, but it explicitly notes the technical challenge of quantifying resource affinities for cross-fed metabolites in vitro. No exact exchanged metabolite was therefore asserted in the record.
