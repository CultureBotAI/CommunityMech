Clostridium acetobutylicum and Clostridium ljungdahlii Carbon-Negative Isopropanol Syntrophic Coculture

Community ID CommunityMech:000480
Ecological State ENGINEERED
Environment bioreactor
ENVO:00002123 · HabitatMech ↗

High-cell-density syntrophic bacterial coculture pairing genetically engineered Clostridium acetobutylicum (CACas9 delta-hbd-p95ace02_atoB, lacking 4-C metabolism and overexpressing an acetone-formation pathway) with the acetogen and ketoreductase partner Clostridium ljungdahlii in a 3.6-L perfusion bioreactor. C. acetobutylicum ferments glucose to acetone, CO2, and H2; C. ljungdahlii consumes dissolved H2—relieving the thermodynamic bottleneck of NADH-driven H2 evolution in C. acetobutylicum—and reduces cross-fed acetone to isopropanol at supratheoretical yields in continuous carbon-negative glucose fermentation.

Syntrophic Hydrogen Scavenging Relieving NADH-Driven Thermodynamic Limitation

SYNTROPHY

Clostridium ljungdahlii scavenges H2 evolved during glucose fermentation by engineered Clostridium acetobutylicum CACas9 Δhbd-p95ace02_atoB, eliminating detectable H2 accumulation and relieving the thermodynamic bottleneck on NADH-driven H2 evolution and acetone production.

High-Cell-Density Continuous Carbon-Negative Isopropanol Fermentation

SYNTROPHY

In pseudo-perfusion and 3.6-L perfusion bioreactors with high cell density and high Clostridium ljungdahlii population fraction, the syntrophic coculture achieves supratheoretical, carbon-negative conversion of glucose to isopropanol as the sole alcohol product.
