Sulfide Spring Autotrophic CPR-Hosting Biofilm

Community ID CommunityMech:000222
Ecological State STABLE
Environment sulfide-rich spring biofilm — ontology label: sulfur spring
ENVO:00000126

An autotrophic biofilm community in sulfide-rich springs sustained by deeply sourced groundwater. Genome-resolved metagenomics combined with bulk geochemistry and scanning transmission X-ray microscopy (STXM) at room temperature and 87 K reveals biofilms dominated by chemolithotrophic sulfur-oxidizing bacteria including Thiothrix and Beggiatoa, with diverse Candidate Phyla Radiation (CPR) bacteria (Gracilibacteria, Absconditabacteria, Saccharibacteria, Peregrinibacteria, Berkelbacteria, Microgenomates, Parcubacteria). STXM imaging shows ultra-small cells adhered near filamentous bacteria, suggesting CPR episymbiosis. Carbon K and sulfur L2,3 edge NEXAFS spectroscopy indicates that filamentous bacteria contain protein-encapsulated elemental sulfur granules consistent with Thiothrix-like sulfur oxidation, and Berkelbacteria and Moranbacteria are predicted to encode a novel electron bifurcation system.

Filamentous Sulfur Oxidation with Encapsulated S Granules

STXM and sulfur-edge spectroscopy show protein-associated elemental sulfur granules in filaments from the MS4 and MS11 spring biofilms, consistent with sulfur oxidation. The authors infer Thiothrix-like and Beggiatoa-related identities from the surrounding community evidence. Putative CPR association with sulfur-oxidizing filaments

HYPOTHESIZED/PARTIAL: small cells occur near or on sulfur-bearing filaments, and the authors propose that some are CPR episymbionts. Attempts to identify CPR cells by FISH were unsuccessful, so the observed cells, their hosts and the fitness consequences remain unresolved. The source/target links retain legacy canonical cross-references for this proposed association; they do not verify a CPR-Thiothrix nivea pair. A possible cryptic sulfur-cycling symbiosis remains a source hypothesis, not demonstrated exchange.

Predicted CPR sulfur and hydrogen metabolic capacity

HYPOTHESIZED/PARTIAL: metagenomic gene content, genomic context and structural models suggest a potentially electron-bifurcating group 3b hydrogenase in Berkelbacteria/Moranbacteria and thiosulfate-conversion capacity in Doudnabacteria. These are lineage-specific predictions, not demonstrated enzyme activity, sulfur/hydrogen flux or a capacity shared by every CPR member. The authors suggest that such capacities could contribute to cryptic sulfur symbioses; neither a particular host nor metabolite exchange was experimentally resolved.
