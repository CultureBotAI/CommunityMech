The 16-member switchgrass (Panicum virgatum) soil bacterial SynCom inoculated onto ten natural inbred Brachypodium distachyon lines grown hydroponically in 0.5x MS, used to link natural variation in root exudates to SynCom composition. Dopamine was one of the most intense LC-MS/MS peaks in roots and root exudates; across lines, exudate dopamine was positively correlated with the relative abundance of Rhodococcus OAS809, Marmoricola OAE513, Rhizobium OAE497, Lysobacter OAE881 and Bosea OAE506 and negatively with Paraburkholderia (Burkholderia) OAS925. In monoculture, 100 ug/mL dopamine stimulated growth of the five positively correlated isolates and inhibited OAS925. SynCom inoculation was associated with lower exudate dopamine. A separate soil microcosm experiment in the same study (agricultural soil amended with dopamine) enriched Actinobacteria; that native soil community is not this record.

Exudate Dopamine Correlates With Six Member Abundances

Across ten host lines, root-exudate dopamine (LC-MS/MS ion intensity) was positively correlated (Spearman, n = 28) with relative abundance of Rhodococcus OAS809, Marmoricola OAE513, Rhizobium OAE497, Bosea OAE506 and Lysobacter OAE881, and negatively with Paraburkholderia OAS925. Correlational: no multiple-comparison adjustment was applied, abundances are relative 16S reads, and dopamine co-varies with other genotype-dependent exudate features.

Participating Taxa: Rhodococcus sp. OAS809, Marmoricola sp. OAE513, Rhizobium sp. OAE497, Lysobacter sp. OAE881, Bosea sp. OAE506, Paraburkholderia sp. OAS925

Dopamine Stimulates Growth of Five Positively Correlated Isolates

In monoculture (R2A or 0.1x R2A, 30 °C), 100 ug/mL dopamine significantly increased final OD600 of Rhodococcus OAS809, Marmoricola OAE513, Rhizobium OAE497, Bosea OAE506 and Lysobacter OAE881, the same members whose abundance correlated positively with exudate dopamine. Single-strain assays: they show a direct dopamine effect on each isolate, not a demonstrated mechanism inside the SynCom, and OD600 cannot distinguish dopamine use as a substrate from signalling.

Participating Taxa: Rhodococcus sp. OAS809, Marmoricola sp. OAE513, Rhizobium sp. OAE497, Lysobacter sp. OAE881, Bosea sp. OAE506

Dopamine Inhibits Paraburkholderia OAS925 Growth

In monoculture, dopamine reduced final OD600 of Paraburkholderia (reported as Burkholderia) OAS925, the only member negatively correlated with exudate dopamine. Single-strain assay; mechanism of inhibition was not examined.

Participating Taxa: Paraburkholderia sp. OAS925

SynCom Colonization Lowers Exudate Dopamine

Across lines, plants inoculated with the SynCom had lower dopamine in spent medium than axenic plants (pooled two-sided t-test). The authors suggest microbial consumption and/or altered plant production or exudation; neither was distinguished, so this is not established catabolism.

Knowledge gaps & discussions (1)

KNOWLEDGE_GAP OPEN Do the five dopamine-stimulated members catabolize dopamine, and does dopamine shift SynCom composition when supplied to the community rather than to single isolates?

The study links exudate dopamine to member abundance by correlation and tests single isolates; it does not supplement the assembled SynCom, measure dopamine depletion by isolates, or use dopamine-deficient plants. The Actinobacteria enrichment was shown in a native soil microcosm, not in this SynCom.
