The persistent contamination of groundwater by trichloroethene (TCE) and its 
highly carcinogenic intermediate vinyl chloride (VC) poses a serious threat to 
drinking water safety and ecosystem health. A critical limitation of many 
microbial approaches is the incomplete dechlorination of VC to ethene. This 
study presents a remediation strategy that integrates biochar derived from 
Zizania latifolia (water bamboo) husks with a native microbial consortium 
enriched in Dehalococcoides mccartyi (Dhc) strain CWV2, a strain capable of 
completely dechlorinating VC to non-toxic ethene. Biochar (BC600) demonstrated 
optimal adsorption while enhancing microbial colonization and activity, leading 
to accelerated TCE degradation and >50 % reduction of VC accumulation. Microbial 
community analysis further indicated that biochar promoted syntrophic hydrogen 
producers and electroactive bacteria, ensuring long-term dechlorination 
stability. This combined strategy not only increases pollutant removal 
efficiency but also reduces the environmental and health risks associated with 
VC accumulation, thereby offering a sustainable pathway for aquifer restoration.
