# Enhancement of gallium recovery from waste LEDs via biogenic lixiviants produced by mixed microbial community
**Authors:** Xiaosheng Zhang, Shoupeng Li, Xiaojian Liao, Qikang Guo, Yueting Zheng, Zhaoyu Leng, Peihong Zheng, Yu Huang, Zihang Liu, Shuiyu Sun
**Journal:** Journal of Environmental Chemical Engineering 13 (2025) 120403
**DOI:** [10.1016/j.jece.2025.120403](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jece.2025.120403)

## Abstract

Waste light emitting diodes (WLEDs) contain gallium(Ga), a critical metal to manufacture semiconductor materials. Due to environmental risk and a worldwide shortage of metals, recycling and recovering these valuable metals have become an urgent task. Bioleaching demonstrates clear technical advantages for gallium recovery from WLEDs. However, the low leaching efficiency remains a major constraint for industrial-scale application of bioleaching. In this study, the differences between contact bioleaching and non-contact bioleaching of WLEDs were evaluated using a mixed microbial community (ratio of Acidithiobacillus thiooxidans, Acidithiobacillus ferrooxidans, Leptospirillum ferrooxidans = 1:1:1). The biogenic lixiviants achieved a Ga leaching efficiency of 99.5 % in 15 g/L pulp density during 3 days of non-contact bioleaching. On the other hand, contact bioleaching led to Ga leaching efficiency of only 45.59 %. The results suggest that the main reason for different Ga leaching efficiencies under contact bioleaching was the bound extracellular polymer substance (B-EPS) of bacteria. B-EPS, particularly the protein component and Polysaccharide, is hypothesized to play a crucial role in high adsorption of metal ions (especially Ga, with an adsorption rate of up to 21.43 %) during contact bioleaching. Compared to bioleaching processes and chemical leaching in previous studies, non-contact bioleaching using biogenic lixiviants almost fully recovered the valuable metal Ga from WLEDs. In addition, the biogenic lixiviants prevented microbial toxicity caused by high pulp density and shortened the leaching time from 10 to 30 days to 3 days. This research provides a promising strategy to recover Ga from WLEDs through non-contact biolixiviant leaching method.
