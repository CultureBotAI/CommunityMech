A study on cadmium immobilization by bacterial consortia: Achromobacter insuavis SL8 and Enterobacter 1 
cancerogenus SL12 2 
1. Detailed methods of soil bacterial isolation and identification. 3 
Each soil sample (1 g) was suspended in 100mL of 0.85% saline solution (pH 7.2), shaken at 28°C and 150 rpm for 2 h to fully dissociate 4 
bacteria from soil particles. The resulting suspension was serially diluted, and aliquots were spread on Luria-Bertani (LB) agar plates (10 g of 5 
tryptone, 5 g of yeast extract, 10 g of NaCl, 20 g agar per liter, pH 7) supplemented with 0.1 mM Cd(II) (as CdCl2), then incubated at 28°C for 6 
one week to isolate single colonies. To assess bacterial Cd(II) resistance, minimum inhibitory concentration (MIC) assays were conducted on both 7 
LB and minimal salts (MS) agar media (10 g of glucose, 2.8 g of Na2HPO4, 1 g of KH2PO4, 0.5 g of (NH4)2SO4, 0.1 g of MgCl2·6H2O, 0.05 g of 8 
CaCl2·4H2O, 0.5 mg of EDTA-2Na, 0.01 mg of FeSO4·7H2O, 0.01mg of ZnSO4·7H2O, 0.003 mg of MnCl2·4H2O, 0.03 mg of H3BO3, 0.02 mg of 9 
CoCl2·6H2O, 0.001mg of CuC12·2H2O, 0.002 mg of NiCl2·6H2O, 0.003 mg of Na2MoO4·2H2O, 15 g agar per liter, pH 7.2) amended with varying 10 
concentrations of CdCl₂. 11 
For strain identification, the 16S rRNA gene sequences of purified isolates were amplified with universal primers 27F (5’ -12 
AGAGTTTGATCCTGGCTCAG-3’) and 1492R (5’-GGTTACCTTGTTACGACTT-3’), followed by sequencing at Tsingke Company (Beijing, 13
