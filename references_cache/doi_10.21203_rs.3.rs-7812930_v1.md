# Stress-adapted bacterial synthetic community enhances drought resilience and recovery in alfalfa

**Authors:** Shamsul Islam Shipar, Taylar Austin, Md. Maniruzzaman Sikder, Raphael Ofoe, Lord Abbey, Cserhati Matyas, Ivana Rajnovic, Rhea Amor Lumactud
**DOI:** [10.21203/rs.3.rs-7812930/v1](https://doi.org/10.21203/rs.3.rs-7812930/v1)

## Introduction

In the present study, we assembled a five-strain bacterial synthetic community (SynCom; Bacillus sp. G3, Bacillus sp. G12, Acinetobacter sp. G13, Plantibacter flavus M251 and M259) from stress-affected environments with complementary plant-beneficial traits, validated its functions in vitro, and tested its performance with alfalfa in non-sterilized soil across well-watered, moderate, and severe drought.

## Methods

Five plant root-associated bacteria (PAB) strains used included Bacillus sp. G3 and G12, and Acinetobacter sp. G13, which were isolated from Poa pratensis grasses growing in drought-prone soils of Southeastern Alberta, Canada, whereas Plantibacter flavus M251 and M259 were recovered from plants inhabiting hydrocarbon-rich oil seep soils in Nova Scotia, Canada.

The isolates were retrieved from -80 degrees C glycerol stocks and streaked onto Luria-Bertani agar (LBA) plates, which were incubated at 28 degrees C for 72 hrs. Resulting colonies were inoculated into 10 mL Luria-Bertani broth (LBB) and cultured with orbital shaking at 125 rpm for 48 hrs.

Drought stress was imposed by maintaining soil moisture at three levels of field capacity: severe drought (SD) at 30%, moderate drought (MD) at 50%, and well-watered (WW) control at 80%.

The soil was then mixed with sand in an approximately 2:1 field soil-sand ratio and transferred to an LDPE-lined, 6x5 in pots.

The SynCom consisted of five bacterial strains ( Bacillus sp. G3 and G12, and Acinetobacter sp. G13, Plantibacter flavus M251 and M259), which were each cultured in LB broth to the exponential phase (OD 600 ~ 0.6), centrifuged at 4000 rpm for 15 mins, and the pellets were resuspended and diluted in sterile distilled water to a standardized OD600 of ~ 0.1.

Equal volumes of the five standardized suspensions were then combined to prepare the SynCom inoculum.

Fifty ml of bacterial suspension was then inoculated into the soil pre-transplantation and drenched into the base of the seedling one week post-transplantation.

## Results

All strains exhibited significantly elevated IAA production compared to the control. Among the five strains tested, only Acinetobacter sp. G13 produced a clear halo around the colony.

All strains exhibited reduced growth with increasing PEG levels, indicating varying degrees of osmotic stress tolerance. P. flavus M251 and M259 maintained the highest growth under 10% PEG. Bacillus sp. G12 and Acinetobacter sp. G13 exhibited moderate drought resilience.

Bacillus sp. G3 and G12 exhibited strong salt tolerance, sustaining growth at concentrations up to 16% NaCl.

Root nodulation occurred only during the recovery phase, where both drought and SynCom had significant effects.

## Discussion

In the present study, SynCom inoculation significantly increased root dry biomass, root volume, root surface area, and root diameter during both stress and recovery phases, indicating that SynCom facilitated lateral root branching and thickening in alfalfa plants.

In the present study, SynCom inoculation significantly increased root nodule formation during the recovery phase.

Some limitations should be acknowledged. SynCom strain dynamics in the rhizosphere were not tracked, so future studies using genome-resolved metagenomics or targeted molecular approaches could link functional persistence to plant outcomes.
