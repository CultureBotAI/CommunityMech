Synthetic microbial communities reveal the mechanisms of fungus-bacterium
metabolic interactions regulating exopolysaccharide production in Viili.

Viili is a viscous fermented milk, whose characteristic texture is primarily
attributed to the abundant production of exopolysaccharides (EPS) by lactic acid
bacteria during fermentation. However, in natural fermentation systems, the
mechanisms by which fungal-lactic acid bacterial interactions regulate EPS
production remain poorly understood. In this study, Viili fermentation was
employed as a model system using a synthetic microbial consortium, consisting of
Lactococcus lactis V9, Lacticaseibacillus paracasei V21, and Geotrichum candidum
Y1, which was constructed using a top-down strategy. Results from supernatant
supplementation, monoculture, and co-culture assays demonstrated that the
introduction of G. candidum markedly promoted the growth of lactic acid
bacteria, resulting in increased quorum sensing activity, and EPS production.
Specifically, the viable lactic acid bacterial counts increased from 8.03 to
8.39 log CFU/mL, when comparing the L. lactis V9 + L. paracasei V21 group with
L. lactis V9+ L. paracasei V21 + G. candidum Y1 group. Furthermore, the
three-species consortium (L. lactis V9 + L. paracasei V21 + G. candidum Y1)
exhibited the highest EPS concentration among all tested microbial combinations,
reaching 1.09 mg/mL. Untargeted metabolomics combined with validation
experiments revealed that G. candidum supplied lactic acid bacteria with
nitrogen sources, including valine. Amino acid depletion and supplementation
experiments indicated that valine, as a key amino acid, is closely associated
with enhanced growth, biofilm formation, and EPS production in lactic acid
bacteria. In addition, co-culture with G. candidum significantly enhanced AI-2
production and luxS gene transcription in lactic acid bacteria. Further analyses
demonstrated that fungal metabolites may contribute to the activation of AI-2
signaling. AI-2 activation and inhibition assays confirmed that AI-2-mediated
quorum sensing positively regulates EPS synthesis. In summary, the results
indicate that G candidum promotes the accumulation of EPS in lactic acid
bacteria through a metabolic interaction mode characterized by nitrogen supply,
quorum sensing activity, and enhanced EPS production. Furthermore, subsequent
analysis revealed that valine, as an important nitrogen source, can promote the
growth of lactic acid bacteria, accompanied by enhanced quorum sensing activity
and EPS production. This study provides a theoretical basis for the controlled
production of functional fermented dairy products.
