Rhizobacterial functional assemblies modulate accumulation of phthalates in choysum (Brassica parachinensis L.)

The two choysum varieties exhibited differential recruitment of bacterial taxa in response to DEHP. The DEHP-sensitive LA V variety preferentially recruited DEHP-degrading bacteria, adopting an "avoidance-like" pattern to minimize DEHP accumulation. In contrast, the DEHP-tolerant HA V variety recruited plant growth-promoting bacteria, prioritizing growth regardless of DEHP accumulation. Synthetic microbial communities (SynComs) constructed from variety-specific isolates, along with corresponding cross-inoculation experiments, confirmed preferential colonization within host plants and the resulting phenotypic outcomes.

Representative isolates from rhizosphere soils of the two choysum varieties were selected for functional characterization, including DEHP-degradation capacity and plant growth promoting (PGP) activities. For DEHP degradation, each isolate was inoculated into mineral salt medium (MSM) containing 100 mg/L DEHP as the sole carbon and energy source, and incubated at 28°C and 180 rpm for 72 h. Residual DEHP was extracted and quantified using GC-MS to calculate degradation efficiency.

The simplified SynComs of LA V (named SynLA V) and HA V (named SynHA V) were designed by selecting representative rhizobacterial isolates, respectively. Before constructing the SynComs, pairwise antagonism assay was employed to examine interactions among candidate isolates according to previously-reported method with slight modification. Briefly, the OD600 of each candidate isolate grown in LB liquid medium was adjusted to 1.0. Then on LB agar plates, 0.5 μL suspension of each isolate was inoculated next to one other isolate at distances of 0.6 cm or 1.2 cm. Pairwise interactions were visually observed after 2-4 days of incubation by the colony growth, and the candidate isolates without inhibiting effects on one another were selected. As shown in Table S2, the selected five members of SynLA V harbored DEHP-degradation rates ranging from 22% to 54% and negligible PGP activity, while the selected SynHA V members presented DEHP-degradation rates ranging from <1% to 14% and potent multi-PGP activity. Subsequently, the suspension of each selected isolate was adjusted with PBS to an OD600 of 0.5, and then the SynLA V or SynHA V were obtained by mixing the isolates in equal ratios.

Surface-sterilized seeds of the two choysum varieties LA V and HAV were germinated in vermiculite for 2 weeks and then transferred into pots with soils contaminated by 100 mg/kg DEHP. Each SynCom suspension was diluted to a final OD600 of 0.2 (approximately 105 CFU/mL) with PBS for inoculation. As shown in Fig. S2, the seedlings of LA V or HA V were inoculated with 5 mL of each SynCom inoculum, respectively.

For the SynLA V inoculation, there were three targeted ASVs (Pseudomonas sp. L29, Sphingomonas sp. L75, and Flavobacterium sp. L11) detected in the rhizosphere soil of LA V, but no targeted ASVs detected in the rhizosphere soil of HA V. Instead, for the SynHA V inoculation, there were five targeted ASVs (Bacillus sp. H297, Enterobacter sp. H20, Massilia sp. H156, Rhodanobacter sp. H323, and Streptomyces sp. H96) detected in the rhizosphere soil of HA V, and two strains (Bacillus sp. H297 and Massilia sp. H156) detected in the rhizosphere soil of LA V.

Notably, inoculation of the two varieties (especially the LA V) with SynLA V significantly reduced rhizosphere soil DEHP residues and plant DEHP accumulation in comparison to uninoculated controls.

All sequencing data have been deposited in the NCBI database under accession number PRJNA1263734.
