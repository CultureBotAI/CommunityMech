# High-efficiency biodegradation of pleuromutilin by natural and artificial microbial consortia.

A natural microbial consortium (En4) was developed in this study to achieve 
efficient degradation of pleuromutilin through gradient enrichment and 
domestication. Community analysis showed that pleuromutilin stress significantly 
reduced microbial diversity, and core communities with functional specialization 
were identified across multiple taxa, primarily at the level of Proteobacteria. 
Notably, bacterial genera that have the potential to degrade pollutants, such as 
Allorhizobium-Neorhizobium-Pararhizobium-Rhizobium and Cupriavidus were 
significantly enriched. Additionally, 14 degrading bacteria were isolated from 
the natural microbial community, and an efficient artificial microbial 
consortium comprising five strains: Stenotrophomonas pavanii, Stenotrophomonas 
maltophilia, Lysinibacillus sphaericus, Lysinibacillus mangiferihumi, and 
Pseudomonas songnenensis, was constructed, achieving a degradation rate of 
91.97%. The metabolic profile of the artificial microbial consortia during the 
degradation of pleuromutilin were studied, and potential degradation products 
and metabolic pathways were proposed. This study clarifies the succession rules 
of microbial communities under pleuromutilin stress, constructs an efficient and 
well-defined artificial degradation microbiota, and provides a theoretical basis 
and strain resources for the microbial remediation of this type of antibiotic in 
the environment.
