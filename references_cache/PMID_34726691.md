# ORT: a workflow linking genome-scale metabolic models with reactive transport codes
**Authors:** Rebecca L Rubinstein, Mikayla A Borton, Haiyan Zhou, Michael Shaffer, David W Hoyt, James Stegen, Christopher S Henry, Kelly C Wrighton, Roelof Versteeg
**DOI:** [10.1093/bioinformatics/btab753](https://doi.org/10.1093/bioinformatics/btab753)
**PMID:** [34726691](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34726691/)

## Content

Genome-scale metabolic models (GSMs) have been widely used to study microbial ecosystems. Yet the dynamic nature of microbial systems remains a challenge for GSMs to predict. Reactive transport codes (RTCs) simulate biogeochemical systems by modeling the mass transfer and biochemical reactions occurring in an environmental system through time and space. Both approaches seek to predict metabolic processes and biogeochemical reactions; however, they operate at different scales and employ distinct model parameterizations. To leverage the distinct advantages of each modeling approach, we developed an automated workflow that iteratively cycles between metabolic modeling software in KBase and the reactive transport code PFLOTRAN. Here, we demonstrate this workflow's ability to predict nitrogen cycling patterns with site-specific insight into the chemical and biological drivers of nitrification and denitrification processes. The approach is applied to environmental metagenomic data from a river system predicted nitrogen cycling patterns with site-specific insight into chemical and biological drivers of nitrification and denitrification processes. GSMs identified additional carbon sources utilized, enabling more precise identification of the biogeochemical limiting factors in the system.
